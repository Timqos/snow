STATTIC 0.9.0-S1 / SLOOP 2.8.5

Manual

This manual describes the new STATTIC with four channels, DR–BS–SY–CH. Projects and backups from the old version on Skiff are not compatible with the new format.

HOME — six pages

A short press of HOME returns to HOME. SELECT changes pages 1–6. ALGORITHM picks the channel in the order DR, BS, SY, CH. Holding HOME opens the SLOOP system menu. On pages 4–6 two lines at the bottom say what the keys do there, with PG n/6, the page number; pages 1–3 show the scene down to the bottom instead, their page number in its corner, and their keys are in the table below. The title row above shows the knob last turned and its value.

When changes take effect

During PLAY, pattern changes, mute, solo and the sequence generators (HOME 2 K1, DR's K1–K4, BS's K4) wait for the start of the next 4/4 bar. A new sound, LFO or effects from HOME 2 K2–K4 on BS, SY and CH starts on the MORPH grid instead and glides in (below). Changes to several channels before the same bar start together. A new sound from HOME 2 K2 on BS, SY or CH enters softly: the channel dips and comes back over about 0.15 s at that bar. On CH, K2 also keeps a new sound from coming out much louder than the presets: a loud one is turned down by its own level trim (the mixer’s VOL stays as you set it). A new pattern starts from its first step. On STOP, changes apply at once. Ordinary manual parameter edits stay live.

MORPH: gliding into a new sound

SEL → MORPH, for each of BS, SY and CH: START (1/16, 1/8, 1/4, BAR; 1/8 by default) is the grid a change from HOME 2 K2–K4 starts on; TIME (OFF, 0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s, 4 s, 1 BAR, 2 BAR; 2 s by default) is how long its values take to glide from where they are to the new ones; CURVE SMOOTH eases in and out like a hand on the knobs, LINEAR moves evenly. Every continuous value glides: tone, hammer, decays, brightness, drive, noise, air, LFO rates and depths, effect sends, feedback, reverb size, the level trim. Switches change at once: waves, FM ratios, reverb models, sync divisions, modulation routing. A value locked on the playing step glides under its lock. Turning again during a glide aims again from where the values are. With TIME OFF a new K2 sound replaces the old at once with the short dip of before. Undo, loads and preset changes always act at once.

Sequences and generation

Each channel starts with 64 steps and has three patterns. The generator writes four bars, with bars two and four varied; bar one may return as bar three. BS makes a bass phrase, SY a melodic line, CH longer harmonic phrases with held notes. Directions: forward, backward, ping-pong and random. EVOLVE renews the phrase every 1, 2 or 4 passes. AMBIENT. EVOLVE → GENERATE picks the generator of BS, SY and CH: AMBIENT (the default for new channels) or ALL, the energetic one of before (NOTES and MELODY keep notes or rhythm). AMBIENT writes few, long, slow notes: BS on the bar’s 1 (sometimes its 3), held to the next; CH a held chord every bar or two on a slow progression, now and then floated off the 1; SY a sparse line of one to three long notes a bar (DENSITY), some bars resting, drawn from a few shapes (drops, a rising arc, an answer a bar later, a high echo). A project saved before keeps its setting: switch it per channel in SEL → EVOLVE. A saved project holds all three patterns of every channel.

The generator settings are in the SEL windows: GENERATE (DENSITY, SYNCOPATE, SPAN, CONTOUR), GEN 2 (COMPLEX, HUMAN, ROTATE, ACCENT), GEN 3 (BASE NOTE, VARIATION, PARM AMT, TARGET), EVOLVE (GENERATE, EVOLVE, direction) and SEED (SEED A–D). TARGET ALL makes a HOME 2 knob act on all four channels.

Parameter locks. PARM AMT sets how many: at 0 K1 makes none. K1 on BS, SY and CH locks CUTOFF/BRIGHT, TONE, PAN or REV (on BS, its FX SEND) on steps that play. K4 on BS replaces all of BS’s locks and keeps the notes: CUTOFF, TONE, DECAY and SWEEP move either way, DRIVE and FX SEND only up, sometimes two on one step; at PARM AMT 0 it still makes a few. On DR each category locks its own steps: kick LENGTH, BEND, DRIVE; snare FILTER, LENGTH, DRIVE; hat FILTER, LENGTH, PAN; percussion PAN, BEND, FILTER. FILTER only closes and DRIVE only adds. A category’s old locks are replaced, so they do not pile up; at PARM AMT 0, DR makes none. A lock acts on its whole step: when two categories play on one step, both hear it. Up to 24 locks per channel.

Drum generator. COMPLEX (GEN 2) sets how far the drums break up; at 0 the plain generator plays. Above 0 the kit takes a feel: half-time (snare on beat 3 with a broken kick around it, more often from 118 BPM), straight (snare on 2 and 4), or the plain pattern. Generated alone, a kick follows the snare already there. Hat and percussion beats may take their own grid: rolls (ratchets ×2 or ×3), eighth-note triplets on nudged steps, ramps from ×1 to ×4 or back, a dragged beat, a gap or a flam. The kick sometimes doubles, never on the downbeat. Now and then the kick plays a short retrig in a triplet grid: three hits in a step (1/48), a two-step 1/32 roll, a 16th-triplet pair (1/24) or eighth triplets (1/12), never on the downbeat. Short bursts of quiet ratchets run on the hats and all eight percussion sounds, and the snare may play a fill at the end of every second bar. The higher COMPLEX, the more of all this. Each generation also takes a mood: mostly SPARSE or LIGHT (fewer hits, rarer rolls and bursts; the kick’s downbeat and the snare’s backbeat stay), sometimes FULL as before. Generating the whole kit gives all four one mood. Hat and percussion may take a figure of their own instead of the plain line: euclidean, polymetric (a cycle of 3, 5, 6, 7 or 9 steps running across the bar lines), a few scattered ticks, off-beat pairs, or two of their sounds answering each other; the eight percussion sounds share the parts between them. The generator remembers the last eight patterns of each category and remakes one that is too close to them. A step’s nudge is shared by every sound on it, so a category nudges only the steps it plays alone.

Sound

BS uses the STATTIC FM engine: FM PAIR, FM CHAIN, FM PAR, RING, ADDITIVE and FOLD, with SINE, TRI, SAW, SQUARE, PULSE and ORGAN waves, oscillator detail, noise, pitch envelope, filter and resonance, sub, feedback routing and key tracking. It plays one note at a time. 15 factory presets: DEEP BASS, RUBBER BASS, HOLLOW BASS, ACID SUB, REESE BASS, PLUCK BASS, FM GROWL, DUB SUB, SOFT SINE, SAW BASS, and five built on the sine wave: SINE SUB, SINE PLUCK, FM SINE, 808 SINE, ROUND SINE.

SY and CH use KEYS, a two-operator FM voice for electric pianos, organs and glassy keys. The carrier is WAVE 1; the modulator is a sine at RATIO, fine-tuned by SHAPE. TONE sets the FM depth, HAMMER how much of it falls away over the FM DECAY time, VELO how much a soft note darkens. HISS adds steady noise and AIR a burst of noise falling over AIR DECAY. BRIGHT is a low-pass filter, DRIVE a soft saturation. The algorithms, feedback, sub, resonant filter and pitch sweep of the BS engine are hidden on these channels. 15 factory presets: RHODES, WURLI, DX PIANO, GLASS KEYS, BELL KEYS, ORGAN, SOFT KEYS, CLAV, LOFI KEYS, PAD KEYS, SINE KEYS, SINE BELL, SINE PAD, SINE PLUCK, PURE SINE.

SY’s 8-bit / FX sounds. SY has five more presets: BLIP, ZAP, NOISE HIT, CHIP ARP and GLITCH, and HOME 2 K2 makes such a sound once in every four turns (blips, zaps falling down the scale, lasers climbing it, noise hits, chip arpeggios, glitches, dealt so the kinds do not repeat; their pitch never slides, every move is a step of the scale). On SY only, four more fields appear: CHIP ARP steps the note through degrees of the track’s scale (1-3-5, 1-5-8, 1-3-5-8, 8-5-3-1, BROKEN), so it stays in key; ARP RATE sets its step (about 6–100 ms); P AMOUNT sweeps the pitch from that many semitones away into the note over P DECAY (which also times AIR). Their level is matched to the keys sounds.

Ambient styles on SY and CH. HOME 2 K2 on SY and CH draws a style first, then the sound inside it: SCAPE (slow attack, held, dark and smooth), DARK KEYS (warm and round), PLUCK (short, a bright hit falling warm), MALLET (marimba, vibes, kalimba), BELL (glass and tines, quiet, shining on top), BLIP (a short pure tick), or, one turn in seven, the wider draw of before. Two sounds of one style still differ: everything the style does not set is drawn as before. On SY each style carries a small level trim so they sit together. On SY and CH, K2 keeps the notes in tune (no fine detune, the FM modulator only on whole harmonics, at most a touch of glide), never makes a click (a sound without much sustain lasts about 0.7–1 s) and lets every note fade out; K3 (G/LFO) never puts an LFO or an envelope on the pitch, so the notes stay in the scale, nor on VELO or DRIVE, so the level stays. A K2 sound's level is evened for its own space: a big reverb or delay, a long feedback or a long sustain take it down a little. On SY and CH, K4 (G/FX) draws delay and reverb anywhere up to about 90 %, with long reverb tails. Each K2 sound on SY and CH also brings its own space, its reverb and delay drawn with it: SCAPE and BELL in a big room, PLUCK, BELL and BLIP into delays, the keys and mallets in between. DR’s K4 (G/FX) draws a softer space for the shared DR/BS rack.

All three synth channels keep the amp envelope, ENV 2, LFO B, four modulation matrix slots and spread. SY plays up to four voices and CH up to three, release tails included; CH’s release starts at 2BAR of the tempo. Voices come from SLOOP’s shared budget of eight, which hands voices over with a short fade when it runs out.

Effects. SY and CH each have their own rack: tape, bitcrush, chorus, delay and reverb. DR and BS share one rack, set in DR’s FX windows: DR plays into it fully, and BS by its FX SEND (in the REVERB slot, after its fader). BS has no rack windows of its own; its DRIVE stays. BYPASS bypasses a channel’s rack (on DR, the shared one; on BS, its send). Every channel keeps DRIVE and the SLOOP INSERT, SLICER, duck, master filter, texture and punch-in effects. WDT covers the channel’s output together with its rack.

All 35 scales

CHROMATIC, MAJOR, MINOR, DORIAN MODE, MIXOLYDIAN, MAJOR PENTATONIC, MINOR PENTATONIC, MINOR HARMONIC, PHRYGIAN, LYDIAN MODE, LOCRIAN MODE, MINOR MELODIC UP, MINOR BLUES, WHOLE TONE, HALF-WHOLE DIMINISHED, WHOLE-HALF DIMINISHED, 8-TONE SPANISH, BHAIRAV, DORIAN #4, HIRAJOSHI, INSEN, IWATO, KUMOI, LOCRIAN SUPER, LYDIAN AUGMENTED, LYDIAN DOMINANT, MAJOR HARMONIC, MESSIAEN 3, MESSIAEN 4, MESSIAEN 5, MESSIAEN 6, MESSIAEN 7, MINOR HUNGARIAN, MINOR MELODIC DOWN, PHRYGIAN DOMINANT.

ROOT sets the tonic. QUANT SNAP ties the keys and MIDI input to the scale; the generators use its degrees. The full scale name is shown under the keyboard diagram in SEL. Chord types: OFF, DIA3, DIA7, MAJ, MIN, DOM7, MAJ7, MIN7, SUS4, POWER. Voicing: CLOSE, OPEN, INV1, INV2, BASS. FOLLOW: KEY, SCALE or FIT, with the ROOT, LOW, HIGH or LAST note of the source.

Other SLOOP windows

A short press of ENV, LFO, FX, SEL, EDIT, ARP or SEQ opens that group of windows. SELECT pages through the group; K1–K4 edit the fields shown. GLO opens the global parameters, tempo, MIDI and other settings. GLO + SELECT changes the tempo. Holding a button gives SLOOP’s own layers: FX — punch-in, EDIT — erase and undo/redo, ARP — note repeat, SEQ — steps, SEL — tonic, GLO — mute/solo/fill/tap tempo, SAVE — song sections.

EDIT + OCT− is undo, EDIT + OCT+ redo, up to eight history entries. GLO + SAVE — temporary save; GLO + HOME — bring back all channels; GLO + OCT+ — bring back the selected channel. GLO + EDIT + K1–K4 — Control All; GLO + EDIT + OCT− adds the selected channel to Control All or takes it out. The temporary save and the history do not survive switching off.

DR: synthesised micro drums

One drum machine with 16 positions and six voices. DR synthesises its sounds: short, dry clicks in the manner of ambient IDM rather than big drums. Each category has its own families: KICK (pips, tocks, sub dots, pops, wood, FM thuds, felt, chip, knuckle, dub dots), SNARE (snaps, glass rims, paper, micro claps, ticks, brushes, chip, FM snaps, cards, dust), HAT (ticks, dust, metal, chip, grain, air, glass, crackle, sizzle, needles) and PERC (blips, drops, wood, bells, FM pings, chip blips, glitches, knocks, tines, bubbles, marimba, claves). A draw (PRESET on HOME 1, HOME 2 K1–K4) gives every position a new sound: a family, and its pitch, length, click, filter and noise varied within a narrow, soft range (no chip or glitch families, no lo-fi). The same draw always sounds the same, so projects, snapshots and undo keep their sounds. Every hit also differs by a few cents and in its click. FILTER, DRIVE, BEND and LENGTH shape them. The open hat is longer, the pedal hat shorter. The previous build with the 40 sampled shots is kept: STATTIC 0.9.0-S1 · 3ed5c69.

User memory: four separate banks of 60 KiB, each with a 512-byte header/reserve and at most 16 zones. IMA ADPCM, mono, 22 050 Hz: one 0.5 s shot is 5513 bytes of audio data, so 11 fit in a bank, 44 in all. Your own shots from the banks join the draw now and then (one draw in four, when the category has some); a user shot that is not available plays the synthesised sound instead.

The sample manager shows the four banks; + Add sounds converts the files (up to 1 s each, the category from the file name) and writes them straight to the FM-1, ✕ deletes a sound. Every write is checked by reading the bank back. A deleted sound’s place stays, so the other sounds keep theirs.

Saving and MIDI

Four projects/sections A–D and a separate autosave of the working project; 32 user presets. A project holds all 12 patterns, the parameters, the chosen shots, the Control All offsets and the drum position settings. Flash is written while the sequencer is stopped and the audio is quiet. Default MIDI channels: BS 1, SY 2, CH 3, DR 10; change them in GLO → MIDI. DR supports GM or a 1–16 mode. MIDI learn, clock in/out, pitch bend, mod wheel, expression, channel aftertouch, sustain and SLOOP remote buttons are kept.

Program Change on BS, SY and CH: values 0–9 pick the channel’s first ten factory presets; 10–41 pick user slots 1–32. The five newest factory presets (11–15) are reached with PRESET on HOME 1. A preset made for another channel type is refused. Some MIDI software shows these values as 1–128, that is, one higher.

USB console and audio check

Press STOP. Hold HOME → AUDIO → USB SERIAL → ON. Close the menu with HOME to keep the setting, switch the FM-1 off completely and unplug USB. Switch it on, connect USB and open the USB console page. The console shows the L/audio_late counter, the synth/player and rack time of each channel, event time, the master buses and the state of the second core. A rising L means a missed audio deadline. A CPU percentage alone does not prove there are no short load peaks.

To test a candidate: a cold start; CH + BS + DR; all four channels; several minutes of playing and randomising, including the drum generator at high COMPLEX. Mark in the console the moment you hear a crackle and save the JSON. Until such a test passes on a physical FM-1, the build stays a candidate.

Install and going back

The STATTIC installer checks the FM-1 model, the package’s integrity and the available update path. Before moving from the old STATTIC/Skiff, Tessera or SLOOP, make a backup in the firmware that created the data. It is not converted to the new architecture automatically. Installing the new STATTIC changes the storage layout; treat old projects and user samples as incompatible.

To go back, use “Install the factory firmware” with the original FM-1 V15 or V16 package that matches the boot code of your device. The installer checks this match; do not force past it. Finish an interrupted update with the same package first. After a successful install, wait for the restart and check the version. If the device does not start a working firmware, you need the built-in UBOOT and the official M‑VAVE utility, the file made for the FM-1 and the manufacturer’s instructions for its hardware revision.
